Overview

As Neo Financial continued to grow, its account experiences needed a refresh.

I led the redesign of chequing, credit, and high-interest savings accounts, bringing them together with the existing GIC experience under a clearer, more consistent design.

The Problem

The old tab-based layout made key information and features harder to find.

Through customer support calls and in-app feedback, we started seeing a few recurring issues:

  • Card management was hard to find across chequing and credit accounts.
  • Credit statements were difficult to locate, leading to customer support calls.
  • Outstanding balances weren't always clear, leaving credit customers unsure of how much they owed.
  • Upcoming features needed a home. Features like joint chequing didn't have a clear place within the existing structure.

It became clear that we needed to rethink how these experiences were organized, not just how they looked. But oh baby, I was going to update the look too, you better believe it 😎

The Approach

I started by looking at how other financial apps approached similar challenges.

I didn't want to reinvent the wheel. I started by looking at how other financial apps, including Wealthsimple, Revolut, and traditional banks, organized their accounts.

I broke down common navigation patterns, how information was prioritized, and how customers accessed everyday actions. From there, I explored how we could bring those ideas together into something that worked for Neo.

The Redesign

01. Making everyday actions easier to find

We moved away from the old tab-based navigation and introduced quick actions directly on the account screen.

Common tasks like paying a credit card, viewing statements, and managing cards became more prominent, while account settings moved to a more familiar location in the header.

Tap the card or settings to explore

02. Making account information easier to understand

Rather than relying on rows of numbers, I explored more visual ways to show important account information.

For credit accounts, this meant introducing indicators for available credit and utilization, helping customers understand their account at a glance.

Tap Current balance, the utilization arrow, or the statement

03. One structure, different accounts

I developed a shared information hierarchy that could work across Neo's financial products, while still giving each account room for its own features.

For example, savings accounts could prioritize interest earnings and savings goals, while credit accounts could focus on payments, statements, and available credit.

04. Making room for what's next

With joint accounts on the horizon, the new structure also needed to accommodate shared ownership.

I explored using member avatars in the account header, borrowing a familiar pattern from collaborative apps to make joint accounts recognizable and provide an entry point for managing members.
